Nashville's Highland Rim Forest Protected, Expanding Beaman Park

The Conservation Fund has secured 300 acres of critical forestland along Nashville's Highland Rim, protecting one of the Southeast's largest intact forest ecosystems from development. Working with the City of Nashville and Metro Nashville Parks, the protected land will expand Alvin G. Beaman Park and serve as a vital refuge for wildlife while buffering the region's water supply. Nashville Mayor Freddie O'Connell noted that forests are infrastructure: "It cools our summers, slows our floodwater, filters our streams, and cleans the air we breathe."

Wildlife Corridor Near Yellowstone Protected from Mining Threat

The Conservation Fund and Greater Yellowstone Coalition have permanently protected 161 acres on Crevice and Palmer Mountains near Yellowstone National Park, closing the door on a threatened gold mine. The protection secures critical habitat for grizzly bears, elk, mule deer, bighorn sheep, and bison, with the deal also transferring 208 acres of mineral rights to prevent future mining in this vital wildlife corridor.

Brazil Achieves Historic Low in Amazon Deforestation

Brazil has slashed Amazon deforestation to its lowest level in 13 years, with satellite alerts detecting just 2,874 sq km of forest loss in the 12 months to July 31—down 55.6% below the decade average. The achievement reflects President Lula's determined efforts to crack down on illegal rainforest clearance through improved enforcement and protection of Indigenous lands. With the momentum building, Brazil is aiming for zero deforestation by 2030, a target campaigners increasingly believe is within reach.

Rare Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle Returned Home After Welsh Rescue

Rhossi, a critically endangered Kemp's ridley sea turtle, has been flown 5,000 miles from Wales back to the Gulf of Mexico after 20 months of rehabilitation. The juvenile was rescued barely alive on a Welsh beach in December 2023 and carefully nursed back to health by staff at Anglesey Sea Zoo, who arranged his return to Padre Island National Seashore in Texas. The rescue mission represents dedicated conservation efforts for one of the world's rarest sea turtles.

California Launches Youth Environmental Council After Federal Program Lapses

California has created the CalEPA Environmental Youth Advisory Council, launching a state-level response after the Trump administration quietly allowed the federal EPA's National Environmental Youth Advisory Council to expire. The new program is accepting applications from ages 14 to 20 to provide young people a direct voice in state climate policy and environmental decision-making, ensuring that those who will inherit climate impacts have a say in solutions being crafted today.

Indigenous Land Restitution in Chile Empowers Mapuche Communities

Researchers studying Chile's Indigenous Land Restitution Program found that returning 510,000 acres to the Mapuche—the country's largest Indigenous group—empowered communities to shape land use according to their own cultural values, with a rise in traditional pasturage and a significant drop in non-native forest plantations. While the study highlights that ownership alone doesn't guarantee environmental gains, it demonstrates how restitution addresses historical dispossession and supports Indigenous self-determination.

English Ospreys Breed for First Time Since Victorian Era

Ospreys have bred successfully at Cumbria Wildlife Trust's Campfield Marsh in northern England for the first time since the Victorian period, rearing three chicks named Ash, Bowness Bobbi, and Beau by local schoolchildren. This remarkable recovery comes after persecution and egg collecting drove the species to extinction as a breeding bird in the UK in the early 20th century, and reflects how habitat protection and dedicated conservation can reverse the fortunes of endangered species.

Lawsuit Challenges Expansion of Livestock Grazing on 24 Million Acres of Public Lands

The Center for Biological Diversity sued the Trump administration over its approval of an expanded livestock grazing plan that would open 24 million acres of public land—an area larger than the state of Maine—to cattle. The plan threatens imperiled species including wolves, grizzly bears, Gunnison sage-grouse, Mexican spotted owls, and spikedace, targeting sensitive landscapes like Canyons of the Ancients National Monument and Sonoran Desert National Monument.
