Today's Briefing
1
Mt. Graham red squirrel remains one of North America's most endangered species

With only about 230 individuals remaining in the wild, the Mt. Graham red squirrel clings to survival on a single mountaintop in southern Arizona. Climate change is threatening the forest habitat these critically endangered creatures depend on, making conservation efforts increasingly urgent. WBUR reports that the species faces mounting pressures as conditions on the mountain shift.

2
Coral Catch's 'Sisterhood' empowers Indonesian women in marine restoration

A unique conservation program on Indonesia's Gili Air island is transforming coral reef restoration while creating career pathways for local women. Coral Catch, founded by marine conservationist Rose Huizenga, has trained 44 women who have deployed over 300 restoration structures and planted 5,000 coral fragments. Every graduate continues working in conservation, demonstrating how Indigenous and community-led initiatives can achieve long-term sustainability and environmental stewardship.

3
H5N1 bird flu poses severe threat to Australia's beloved native bird species

Waterbirds across Australia face heightened risk from the deadly H5N1 virus, which has already caused mass mortalities among greater crested terns. The Guardian reports that iconic species like black swans, pelicans, and the Australian fairy tern are particularly vulnerable due to their breeding and migration patterns. Experts warn that conservation measures, including vaccination programs, are critical to protect wildlife populations.

4
Jackie the bald eagle's legacy inspires $10 million conservation effort in California

Thousands mourned the loss of Jackie, the world's most famous bald eagle who lived on Big Bear Lake in southern California. Her livestream brought millions of people closer to wildlife and inspired Friends of Big Bear Valley to raise $10 million to permanently conserve 63 acres of critical habitat, including the lake's last undisturbed shoreline. Jackie's legacy demonstrates the power of connecting the public directly to endangered species for conservation impact.

5
Federal Endangered Species Act rule change jeopardizes habitat protections nationwide

The Trump administration finalized a rule in July that dramatically narrows the definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act, weakening protections for critical wildlife habitat. The change allows oil drilling, mining, and logging in protected areas as long as animals aren't directly injured or killed. Environmental law experts warn that species like Vermont's Canadian lynx and rare plants depend on habitat protection to recover, making the rollback a significant threat to endangered species conservation.

6
Amazon deforestation hits lowest rates since 2016, offering conservation hope

The Brazilian Amazon experienced lower deforestation rates from January to June 2026, with 500 square miles cleared—a 38% decrease compared to the same period last year. This reduction marks the lowest figure since 2016, suggesting that sustained conservation efforts and Indigenous land stewardship are yielding results. Experts say continued commitment to protecting rainforest habitat and supporting Indigenous communities is essential to prevent backsliding on this conservation progress.

7
Coalition defends Big Bend National Park from border wall construction

Conservation groups filed a legal brief to stop border wall construction in Big Bend National Park, protecting critical wildlife habitat and one of America's most remote wilderness areas. The Coalition to Protect America's National Parks argues that construction would damage irreplaceable ecosystems and violate environmental law. The effort highlights ongoing tensions between security infrastructure and species protection in the American West.

8
Free entrance weekends return to U.S. National Parks in celebration of America's conservation heritage

National Park Week (August 22–30, 2026) invites Americans to explore 433 national park sites with free entry, celebrating America's commitment to preserving wildlife and biodiversity. Parks from the Great Smoky Mountains to the California coast offer visitors opportunities to experience irreplaceable natural habitat. The annual tradition reinforces public support for conservation and reminds citizens of the value of protecting these lands for future generations.
