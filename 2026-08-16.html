Phoenix Species Project Launches with $200M to Recover 100 Threatened Species

The Phoenix Species Project, backed by Leonardo DiCaprio and Jeff Bezos, launched on August 4 with a $200 million commitment to support the recovery of 100 threatened species across 30 countries. The ambitious initiative targets mammals, birds, plants, fish, and amphibians facing extinction, combining funding with scientific expertise and on-the-ground conservation action. This coordinated investment represents a significant shift toward scalable, international species recovery efforts that can compete with development pressures.

2
ESA Harm Rule Rescission Threatens Florida's Federally Protected Wildlife and Water Quality

As the Trump administration's harm rule rescission takes effect September 14, Florida's endangered species—including manatees, reticulated flatwoods salamanders, and gopher tortoises—face unprecedented vulnerability. The removal of habitat destruction as actionable "harm" under the Endangered Species Act enables developers to circumvent environmental protections while allowing state agencies to roll back water quality regulations linked to species deaths. Florida's Department of Environmental Protection is already weaponizing the change, appealing a landmark ruling that required pollution controls to protect manatee seagrass habitat.

3
Global Marine Protected Areas Fall Short on Shark and Ray Conservation Despite 30x30 Goal

New research from Mongabay reveals that marine protected areas (MPAs) worldwide inadequately safeguard sharks and rays from overfishing and habitat destruction. While 196 countries committed to the '30×30' goal—protecting 30% of ocean area by 2030—only 10% of the ocean currently benefits from protected status. The analysis underscores that most existing MPAs lack the size, enforcement, and governance needed to truly protect elasmobranch populations that anchor ocean ecosystem health.

4
Western Spadefoot Toad Gains Emergency Protections as California Climate Pressures Intensify

The California Fish and Game Commission accepted a petition on August 13 to consider listing the western spadefoot toad as a threatened or endangered species, granting the desert amphibian critical take protections. Once abundant across the southwestern United States, this rare toad now faces rapid habitat loss from agricultural conversion, urban sprawl, and intensifying drought cycles linked to climate change. The species' near-complete disappearance from historical ranges highlights the cascade of pressures small specialists face in rapidly changing ecosystems.

5
Deforestation Extends Amazon Dry Season by Up to 5 Weeks, Pushing Forest Toward Collapse

Three major studies published in Nature and PNAS between April and August 2026 reveal that Amazon deforestation has already extended the dry season by four to five weeks annually in the most affected regions, pushing the rainforest toward a critical rainfall collapse tipping point. Deforestation rates fell 38% in the first half of 2026, yet this reprieve remains insufficient to reverse ecosystem-level changes already underway. Scientists warn that protecting the world's largest tropical rainforest requires sustained commitment far beyond current efforts.

6
Wildlife Corridors Boost Movement Across Habitat by 50 Percent, Reshaping Conservation Strategy

New research demonstrates that wildlife corridors—connected networks of habitat linked by culverts, bridges, and restored passages—increase wildlife movement between fragmented areas by approximately 50 percent compared to unconnected landscapes. Florida and Western states are scaling investment in safe infrastructure designed specifically to reduce vehicle collisions while maintaining genetic diversity in increasingly isolated populations. Habitat connectivity now ranks alongside habitat protection as a cornerstone strategy in preventing species extinction in human-dominated landscapes.

7
Seagrass Meadows Emerge as Climate Solution Storing Blue Carbon While Supporting Marine Life

Seagrass ecosystems are experiencing renewed conservation attention as researchers quantify their extraordinary capacity to sequester blue carbon while simultaneously supporting crucial fish nurseries and filter-feeding species. The hidden meadows absorb nutrient pollution that drives harmful algal blooms and provide shelter for seahorses, turtles, and crustaceans. Coastal restoration projects prioritizing seagrass recovery offer a rare win-win: climate mitigation coupled with biodiversity recovery in the ocean's most productive yet vulnerable habitats.
