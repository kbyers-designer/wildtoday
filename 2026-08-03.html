Ethiopia's Massive Tree-Planting Campaign Targets 50 Billion Trees by Year-End

In July 2026, Ethiopia launched an ambitious national campaign to plant 700 million trees per day, with the goal of planting 50 billion trees by the end of 2026. This unprecedented reforestation effort represents a major climate action commitment and habitat restoration initiative. The campaign combines efforts across government, communities, and organizations to combat deforestation and build ecological resilience in the face of climate change.

Western Amazon Biosphere Reserves Prevent Loss of 7,400 Square Kilometers of Forest

Biosphere reserves in the Western Amazon—including reserves in Bolivia, Ecuador, and Peru—have collectively prevented the loss of more than 7,400 square kilometers of forest over the past two decades, according to research from the Institute of Environmental Science and Technology. Critically, buffer and transition zones where Indigenous peoples maintain territorial rights proved more effective at reducing deforestation than strictly protected core areas, demonstrating that Indigenous stewardship and participatory governance models are essential to forest conservation.

Green Sea Turtles Downlisted from Endangered—A Conservation Success Story

Green sea turtles have made a remarkable recovery, with populations rising significantly since the 1970s after being hunted to near-extinction for turtle eggs. Their conservation status was downlisted from endangered to least concern in the IUCN's 2025 Red List update, marking a major species recovery milestone. The success resulted from coordinated global efforts including egg protection, hatchling release programs, and reducing fishing net capture.

Namibia's Rarest Plant on the Brink—Living Stone Species at Critical Risk

Lithops werneri, a pebble-like succulent found only in a 4 square-kilometer area in Namibia's Erongo Mountains, was listed as critically endangered in February 2026 with fewer than 200 plants remaining in the wild. The species has declined more than 80% since 1981 due to illegal poaching for the horticulture market and increasingly severe climate change impacts including droughts that its shallow root system cannot withstand. With its entire population in a single location, even one extreme disturbance could extinguish the species entirely.

Texas Dunes Sagebrush Lizard Loses Federal Endangered Protection Amid Oil-Gas Pressure

The Trump administration has vacated federal endangered species protection for the dunes sagebrush lizard in Texas following a lawsuit settlement with Attorney General Ken Paxton. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service was forced to reassess the 2.5-inch reptile's status, with critics arguing the move prioritizes oil and gas development over species conservation. This follows similar rollbacks including removal of endangered protection for the lesser prairie chicken in February 2026.

India's Finless Porpoises Found Far More Numerous Than Thought—Acoustic Monitoring Crucial

Acoustic surveys in India's coastal waters detected Indo-Pacific finless porpoises four times more often than visual surveys, revealing the species is far more prevalent than previously estimated. Researchers at IISER Tirupati and Cornell University found that echolocation-based monitoring is essential for tracking these elusive marine mammals in turbid waters. The porpoises face deadly entanglement in fishing nets and increasing ocean noise that impairs their ability to communicate and locate food.

Indonesia's New Capital Transforms Rainforest While Indigenous Balik Preserve Heritage

As Indonesia constructs its new capital, Nusantara, in the mountains of East Kalimantan in Borneo, the Indigenous Balik community struggles to preserve their cultural heritage and connection to the forest soundscape. A new documentary, Sound Guardians, captures how scientists and local residents are recording the forest's acoustic ecology as chainsaws interrupt the natural sounds that have guided Balik traditions for generations. Community members fear losing younger generations' connection to forest knowledge as development accelerates.
