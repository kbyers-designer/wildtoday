US Policy - Species Protection

Florida Democrats Push Back Against Trump Administration's Endangered Species Act Rollback

Florida's congressional delegation is demanding the Trump administration withdraw regulations that weaken protections under the Endangered Species Act, which eliminate habitat degradation from the definition of "harm." The change threatens over 100 Florida species including manatees and Florida panthers, with Florida manatees particularly vulnerable after 1,255 animals died from pollution-caused algae blooms in the Indian River Lagoon between 2020-2022. The Florida Wildlife Federation warns the rollback will accelerate coastal development and weaken decades of collaborative conservation efforts, calling Florida "ground zero" for these changes given rapid urbanization threatening critical wildlife habitat.

Environmental Protection - Toxic Chemicals

Environmental Groups Urge EPA to Reject New 'Forever Chemical' for Datacenter Cooling

A coalition of 17 environmental groups has submitted formal comments demanding the EPA reject Chemours' request to fast-track approval for Opteon 2P50, a new PFAS "forever chemical" intended to cool datacenters. The groups warn the chemical is likely more toxic than claimed and poses unreasonable health risks—PFAS compounds are linked to cancer, liver disease, kidney issues, and birth defects—while also presenting climate risks. The effort represents a critical moment in protecting communities from toxic industrial pollution, as US drinking water is already widely contaminated with PFAS that accumulate in the body and environment and never break down naturally.

Deforestation & Climate - International

Brazil Deploys Forensic 'Gold Fingerprinting' to Combat Illegal Amazon Mining

Brazilian authorities are using forensic science to trace the origins of seized gold through chemical analysis, targeting illegal mining operations destroying the Amazon rainforest. The technique, known as "gold fingerprinting," examines trace elements like silver and copper to determine where gold was actually mined, helping identify whether supposedly legal gold was extracted from protected forests and Indigenous territories. Amazon Mining Watch estimates 496,000 hectares of rainforest have been cleared for mining since 2023, including severe damage through mercury pollution that contaminates waterways and fish. The forensic strategy supports broader efforts to expose criminal supply chains and protect the Amazon's critical role in storing carbon and regulating global climate.

Indigenous Stewardship - Habitat Restoration

Yurok Tribe Reclaims 125-Acre Gateway as Save the Redwoods League Returns Ancestral Land

Save the Redwoods League transferred 125 acres of land in the heart of Yurok ancestral territory back to the Yurok Tribe, returning a severely degraded former lumber mill site that is now a thriving ecosystem. The 'O Rew Redwoods Gateway and Restoration Project restored one mile of braided stream channel, 20 acres of wetlands, and 20 acres of upland habitat, with the Yurok Revegetation Crew planting nearly half a million native seedlings spanning 50 species. Endangered coho salmon, along with great blue herons, bears, elk, condors, and riparian predators, are already using the restored habitat, representing one of the few large-scale stream restoration projects in rural Northern California where communities can witness ecological recovery powered by Indigenous land management practices.

Indigenous Sovereignty - Water & Salmon

California Approves Historic Tribal Land Return for Quartz Valley Indian Reservation

The California State Water Resources Control Board approved $25 million in financing to help the Quartz Valley Indian Reservation acquire nearly 40,000 acres of Scott River headwaters in Northern California's Klamath River Basin—what could become the second largest tribal land return in state history. The acquisition returns ancestral lands with salmon-bearing streams, cultural sites, forests, and critical drinking water resources to tribal stewardship, enabling the Quartz Valley Tribe to restore salmon habitat, improve forest health, and strengthen climate resilience while expanding opportunities for cultural access and ecological restoration. Quartz Valley Reservation Chairman Leonard Lincoln called it a "monumental victory" for a tribe that was once terminated and confined to just 2,000 acres.

Invasive Species - Biodiversity Protection

Asian Hornet Invasion in Guernsey Prompts New Community-Based Tracking Strategy

Residents of Guernsey and Herm are being asked to "track not trample" Asian hornets in a new community-based conservation effort targeting the invasive species that poses major threats to island biodiversity and honeybees. More than 200 Asian hornet queens have been caught so far this year, with nine primary nests destroyed across the island. The non-native species, which arrived in Europe after hitching rides in pottery shipments from Asia and first appeared in Guernsey in 2017, can expand rapidly during August and September into nests holding up to 2,000 hornets. The Track Don't Trample strategy enlists volunteers to photograph hornets and note travel direction before submitting information, allowing authorities to locate and destroy nests safely while protecting communities and wildlife from this aggressive predator.

Urban Conservation - Species Recovery

Urban Biodiversity Thrives as Hull City Center Creates 'Butterfly City' in Wildflower Meadow

A patch of land in Hull city centre has been allowed to grow wild, creating a haven for insects that has attracted ten species of butterflies including rare small skipper butterflies now breeding in the meadow. The "mini meadow" established in 2023 near St Stephen's Shopping Centre demonstrates that cities can support thriving ecosystems when given the chance—the site has also become home to dragonflies and grasshoppers, creating valuable habitat for caterpillar-dependent species that require uncut grass throughout the year. Hull Butterfly City advocates explain that while flowering plants are essential for adult pollinators, most butterfly species are highly specific about host plants for their caterpillars, making habitat diversity crucial for species recovery in otherwise developed urban areas.
