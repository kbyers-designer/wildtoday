DiCaprio and Bezos Launch $200M Global Initiative to Save 100 Critically Endangered Species

Leonardo DiCaprio's Re:wild and the Bezos Earth Fund have launched the Phoenix Species Project, a historic $200 million effort to revive 100 of Earth's most threatened species from the brink of extinction. The largest single fund ever dedicated exclusively to species recovery will support conservation of animals including golden-crowned sifakas from Madagascar, Visayan warty pigs from the Philippines, pink land iguanas from the Galápagos, and pangolins across Southeast Asia. The initiative combines resources with local governments, environmental organizations, and Indigenous communities to provide long-term, sustained funding for species on the edge of extinction.

Vietnam's Coffee Boom Erases Rainforests the Size of Luxembourg—Threatening Endangered Wildlife

Vietnam's rise as the world's largest robusta coffee producer has come at a devastating cost: forests the size of Luxembourg have been cleared since 1990 to expand plantations across the Central Highlands. A major investigation reveals that 207,428 hectares of tropical forest were destroyed for coffee production, driving one-third of regional forest loss and pushing endangered species like tigers, elephants, saola, and sun bears into ever-shrinking habitat fragments. Climate change compounds the crisis, as degraded soils and disrupted water cycles now threaten coffee cultivation itself, while smallholder farmers remain trapped in a system that rewards forest destruction and unsustainable practices.

Trump Administration Opens U.S. Ocean Sanctuaries to Commercial Fishing—Pristine Ecosystems at Risk

The Trump administration is lifting protections from some of America's last pristine ocean ecosystems, opening the nation's largest conservation areas in the Pacific Ocean—tiny islands that serve as biodiversity epicenters—to commercial fishing. The remote archipelagos and marine sanctuaries have remained largely untouched, but new federal rules will permit industrial-scale fishing that could devastate unique wildlife and disrupt the delicate balance of ecosystems found nowhere else on Earth. After conservation groups challenged the rollback, a federal judge ordered the administration to conduct a full regulatory process before finalizing the changes.

Colombia's National Parks Show Success Against Deforestation Despite Amazon Pressures

Colombia reported mixed conservation results for 2025: while overall deforestation increased slightly to 119,483 hectares, forest loss in national parks fell nearly 19% to 9,388 hectares, demonstrating the effectiveness of targeted protection strategies. The country has now surpassed its goal of reducing deforestation 20% below 2021 levels, with President Gustavo Petro's landmark cattle traceability law and community conservation incentives showing measurable impact. However, the Amazon remains under intense pressure, with pressure from illegal land grabbing, cattle ranching, and road construction continuing to threaten this critical carbon sink and biodiversity hotspot.

Federal Endangered Species Rule Stripped of Habitat Protections—States Unprepared to Fill the Gap

The Trump administration finalized a major rollback to the Endangered Species Act, eliminating federal protection for habitats where endangered species live by narrowing the definition of "harm" to only direct injury or death. The new rule opens forests, wetlands, deserts, and other critical ecosystems to development, logging, mining, and oil and gas drilling. More than 350,000 public comments opposed the change, and legal experts warn that most states lack laws to prevent habitat destruction, raising fears that species recovery efforts across the nation will stall without federal safeguards.

Indigenous Elder's Urban Forestry Farm Fights Cuts to Native Conservation Programs

Rose High Bear, an Alaskan Native founder of Elderberry Wisdom Farms near Salem, Oregon, is building a model for sustainable agriculture rooted in Traditional Ecological Knowledge—but faces an uncertain future as federal environmental funding shrinks. The farm lost $749,000 in USDA grants in February 2025 due to budget freezes, and a $350,000 initiative supporting young Indigenous and people-of-color urban foresters was never restored. High Bear's program combines urban forestry, native plant nurseries, and micro-enterprises while teaching younger generations ancestral land stewardship practices—work she believes is vital as climate pressures mount.

International Pledges for Shark and Ray Protection—Zanzibar and Madagascar Lead Ocean Stewardship

Zanzibar and Madagascar announced groundbreaking protections for sharks and rays, while Fiji and Panama committed to reducing impacts from commercial fishing and deep-sea operations. The coordinated pledges, announced at an international ocean conference, represent a global recognition that these apex predators are critical to healthy marine ecosystems and fishing economies. The commitments echo warnings from marine scientists that without strong protections, overfished shark populations will collapse, destabilizing entire ocean food webs.

Brazil Sustains Lowest Amazon Deforestation in Decades—Enforcement and Community Support Drive Results

Brazil continues hitting record-low deforestation milestones in the Amazon, with forest loss dropping 68% in April 2026 compared to the prior year. The dramatic progress reflects the Lula administration's combined strategy of expanded federal enforcement against illegal logging operations, support for Indigenous-led land protection, and community-based conservation incentives. Scientists credit the results to political will and consistent resourcing of anti-deforestation enforcement, demonstrating that the Amazon's fate is not inevitable but responds to deliberate conservation action.
