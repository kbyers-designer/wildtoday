North Carolina Invests $10.2 Million in Wildlife Crossings to Protect Bears and Reduce Highway Collisions

North Carolina's legislature and Governor Josh Stein made a historic commitment to wildlife safety by allocating $10.2 million for wildlife crossings and mitigation strategies across the state, with funding recurring every two years. The investment will protect iconic species like black bears and elk in regions including Great Smoky Mountains National Park, using strategically placed underpasses, culverts, and overpasses to help animals safely cross highways. According to NPCA, 81% of Americans and 80% of hunters support continued investment in wildlife crossings to reduce costly collisions and improve animal resilience in connected landscapes.

Miami-Dade County Plans Historic Transfer of 17,000 Acres to Expand Big Cypress National Preserve

Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava announced a landmark proposal to transfer over 17,000 acres of county land—including former immigrant detention facilities—to the National Park Service for permanent protection within Big Cypress National Preserve and the Everglades ecosystem. The conservation landscape is home to endangered species including the Florida panther, ghost orchid, and diverse habitats crucial for Florida's biodiversity. NPCA called the initiative a once-in-a-generation opportunity to protect unique ecosystems and support Indigenous and local stewardship of one of America's most vital natural treasures.

Guernsey Implements 'Track Don't Trample' Strategy as Asian Hornets Threaten Island Biodiversity

Guernsey has asked islanders to report Asian hornet sightings rather than attempt to destroy the invasive species, launching the Track Don't Trample strategy after more than 200 queens were captured on the Channel Islands in 2026. The non-native, invasive species—which arrived in Europe in 2004 via pottery shipments from Asia—poses a major threat to native honeybees and island biodiversity, with the insects comprising up to 39% of their diet on average. Local volunteers have been critical to the tracking effort, allowing authorities to locate and safely destroy primary nests before they expand to hold thousands of hornets.

Ghana Establishes West Africa's Toughest Methane Rules for Oil and Gas, Setting Regional Climate Model

Ghana's Environmental Protection Authority finalized comprehensive Technical Requirements for Methane Inspection and Monitoring, creating one of the world's most rigorous methane regulatory frameworks for oil and gas operations across the entire industry value chain. The framework translates Ghana's Paris Agreement and Global Methane Pledge commitments into measurable, enforceable field practices covering onshore and offshore facilities, gas processing plants, and refineries. The Climate and Clean Air Coalition and Clean Air Task Force supported the effort, demonstrating that ambitious methane regulation is achievable for all economies and establishing a model other West African nations can replicate to rapidly reduce global warming.

Wildlife Advocates Sue Trump Administration Over Endangered Species Act Harm Redefinition

Conservation organizations represented by Earthjustice filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration over a newly finalized rule that removes habitat destruction from the legal definition of harm under the Endangered Species Act, threatening recovery efforts for more than 600 species from salmon to grizzly bears. The redefinition—set to take effect next month—would allow forest clearing, wetland drainage, and mining operations to proceed without federal protection for wildlife habitats where endangered species live. Florida Democrats and conservation advocates warned the change could result in extinction for hundreds of species including manatees and sea turtles, while 85% of Americans and 82% of hunters support the importance of the Endangered Species Act for species protection.

Banks Provide $31 Billion in Sustainable Loans to Palm Oil Companies Linked to Deforestation

Global Witness analysis reveals that major international banks—including Standard Chartered and HSBC—provided $31 billion in sustainability-linked loans to palm oil companies facing allegations of deforestation and corruption in Indonesia between 2018–2025. Wilmar International alone received $950 million in such loans while satellite analysis suggests its concessions may be tied to 4,200 hectares of forest loss after its no deforestation policy's 2015 cutoff date—an area the size of Oxford. Investors including BlackRock hold $46 million in Wilmar stocks through funds labeled ESG or sustainable, highlighting how weak voluntary standards and poor regulatory oversight allow companies to access green finance while ecosystems and Indigenous communities—particularly Minangkabau peoples—lose land and livelihoods to plantation expansion.

Federal Court Halts Trump Administration Bid to Open U.S. Ocean Sanctuaries to Commercial Fishing

A federal judge ordered the Trump administration to conduct a full regulatory process before finalizing changes to open some of America's most pristine ocean sanctuaries in the Pacific to commercial fishing. The remote archipelagos in the nation's largest conservation areas have remained largely untouched and serve as biodiversity epicenters home to unique species found nowhere else on Earth. Following the court's preliminary injunction, conservation groups expressed hope that the judicial review would protect these delicate ecosystems from industrial-scale fishing that could devastate marine wildlife and disrupt ecological balance in areas essential to ocean health.

Britain Pledges Early Action on Deforestation Finance Amid Global Palm Oil Crisis

The UK government committed to carrying out a review of the financial sector's role in driving illegal deforestation and announced it will apply EU Deforestation Regulation standards in Northern Ireland by the end of 2026, with plans to align broader trade rules across Great Britain. The commitment comes as deforestation in Indonesia surged 66% from 2024–2025, driven by expansion of palm oil plantations and government programs that threaten Indigenous lands and intact forests across Borneo and Sumatra. With nearly 2.4 million hectares of intact forest remaining within Indonesia's existing palm oil concessions, UK regulators and environmental organizations say swift action is critical to prevent a fire sale of the world's rainforests and protect vulnerable ecosystems before momentum toward forest protection is lost.
