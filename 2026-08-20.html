1
Amazon deforestation drops to decade-low as Lula's conservation efforts bear fruit

Brazil's Amazon rainforest experienced its lowest deforestation rates since accurate records began in 2016, with 2,874 square kilometers cleared between August 2025 and July 2026—a 37% reduction compared to the previous year. President Lula da Silva's bold environmental policies and Indigenous land stewardship have reversed the damage inflicted during the Bolsonaro administration, demonstrating that sustained conservation commitment can produce measurable results. Scientists warn that continued momentum is essential, as the Amazon remains perilously close to a critical tipping point that would transform the world's largest rainforest into savanna.

2
Trump administration redefines 'harm' under Endangered Species Act, weakening habitat protections

The Trump administration eliminated the regulatory definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act, narrowing how habitat modification is legally interpreted and allowing oil drilling, mining, and logging in protected areas to proceed more easily. The change also ends the automatic application of stronger endangered species protections to newly listed threatened species, requiring individual assessments instead. Conservation experts warn the policy shifts prioritize economic interests over species recovery, jeopardizing the survival of animals like the Canadian lynx and rare plants that depend critically on habitat protection.

3
H5 bird flu confirmed in Australia's little penguin population on Phillip Island

Australia confirmed its first case of deadly H5 bird flu in a little penguin on Phillip Island, home to the world's largest colony of the species with approximately 40,000 birds. Victoria's government has launched an emergency vaccination campaign vaccinating up to 5,000 little penguins across protected populations, marking the world's largest bird flu vaccination program of wild birds. Environmental advocates worry the virus may spread through Australia's diverse and vulnerable wildlife populations, with conservation officials calling for increased support and resources to help species survive the outbreak.

4
Endangered Hawaiian monk seal dies entangled in fishing net off Oʻahu

A three-month-old endangered Hawaiian monk seal was tragically found dead entangled in an abandoned gill net off Oʻahu's North Shore, highlighting the devastating threat that fishing gear poses to Hawaii's threatened and endangered marine wildlife. Hawaiian monk seals are among the world's rarest seal species, and the loss of such a young animal is especially devastating to conservation efforts. Authorities are investigating the abandoned net and urging the public to report any evidence of irresponsible fishing practices that endanger marine life.

5
Blue and fin whales are returning to the southeastern Atlantic where whaling nearly wiped them out

After decades of commercial whaling devastation, blue whales and fin whales are appearing with increasing frequency in the southeastern Atlantic, offering cautious signs of species recovery and demonstrating the power of international conservation agreements. These magnificent marine mammals, once hunted to near-extinction, are reclaiming waters they had abandoned for generations. The recovery underscores the importance of continued marine protection policies and international cooperation to allow whale populations to rebuild their numbers.

6
Arizona's wildlife crossings are helping save endangered desert bighorn sheep from highway collisions

Three strategically designed wildlife crossings over U.S. Highway 93 in Arizona are successfully helping desert bighorn sheep safely traverse the roadway without risking fatal collisions, demonstrating innovative habitat connectivity solutions for endangered species. The crossings allow these vulnerable populations to access critical forage and water sources while avoiding the dangers of traffic. This project shows how thoughtful infrastructure design can balance human transportation needs with species protection and wildlife conservation.

7
Europe's marine protected areas face legal battle to enforce existing conservation laws against destructive fishing

The newly formed MPA Legal Coalition, an alliance of European environmental organizations, is pursuing strategic legal action to enforce existing European Union conservation laws within marine protected areas where bottom trawling and other destructive fishing practices continue unchecked. Despite strong EU nature laws, many designated marine reserves fail to protect the habitats and species they were created to safeguard, allowing heavy industrial fishing gear to damage fragile ecosystems. The coalition's efforts highlight the urgent need to strengthen enforcement and ensure that marine protection designations deliver genuine ocean conservation results.

8
Rescue efforts alone cannot save orangutans; habitat protection essential for species survival

On World Orangutan Day, conservation experts emphasize that rescue and rehabilitation programs, while vital, cannot address the root cause of orangutan population decline: the wholesale loss of rainforest habitat across Southeast Asia. Orangutan populations face catastrophic threats from deforestation driven by palm oil production and logging, making habitat preservation the critical priority for long-term species survival. Indigenous communities and local stewardship must be central to any effective strategy to protect these critically endangered great apes and their forest home.
